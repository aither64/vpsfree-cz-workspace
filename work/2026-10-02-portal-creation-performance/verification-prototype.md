## Measurements and evidence

`result.json` is the authoritative harness result. Exit 0 means its full gate
passed; exit 2 means a timing-only/reduced-history run or unmet timing gate;
exit 1 means incomplete execution/assertion failure. A failed model observation
does not erase the already saved `*-ready.json` timing evidence.

- `history.json`: thread count, total rollout bytes, synthetic shape and setup
  duration. Defaults match the observed active history count, not its content.
- `events/<slug>.jsonl`: every forwarded stage with its emitter duration,
  monotonic observation time, UTC time, CLI boundaries and HTTP phase changes.
- `creation-sample-*-result.json`: durable receipt acceptance/ready times,
  request-to-observed-ready duration, all stages, root/member IDs, first
  assistant time and model completion. Nested stage durations are not summed.
- `creation-*-before-retry.json`, `fault.json`, `fault-events.jsonl`: actual
  injection point and partial state. The original receipt and its attempt
  increment are retained.
- `processes.json`, `seed-process.json`: launched process identities;
  `app-server.log`, `portal.log`, setup `*.stderr.log`: private diagnostics.
  Application receipt/journal/manifest/roster files remain in the private root.

Acceptance-to-ready uses the portal's durable `startedAt` and ready receipt
`updatedAt`. A separate monotonic measurement includes the POST and 100 ms
status polling. Model response time comes from the isolated rollout timestamp,
not a readiness assumption. A response can precede ready; its signed offset
from ready is retained. Warm-up, measured runs and fault cases are separate.

## Actual limits and fallback

The synthetic history has the observed active-file count and configurable
payload size, but uses same-day developer-only rollouts, with no production
turn distribution or archived history. It establishes scaling against many
real persisted files, not equivalence to every production workload. Retain the
planned actual-history portal canary after deployment as a separate gate.

The lost response is at the existing Go-helper/Ruby boundary after a real
`thread/start`, not on the App Server network transport. Once that helper
disconnects, Codex may unload an unmaterialized root. A retry may legitimately
replace that ID; the harness records which outcome occurred and refuses a
replacement if the old root has materialized history. A network-level lost
reply while another client keeps the memory-only root alive remains covered by
focused protocol fixtures, not this prototype. Adding a WebSocket fault proxy
would be a larger fixture and needs the parent's explicit design decision.

The member helper may advance after writing a completion frame before the
harness can kill its exact PID. The actual persisted roster must contain both
a ready member and an unfinished member, or the case is **inconclusive**. It
never labels a fully completed team as a partial-creation test. An uncertain
pending member may also refuse recovery correctly; the harness reports that
failure for parent diagnosis rather than clearing its reservation or retrying
repeatedly. The application is not modified to make injection easier.

No benchmark fork or approved-plan HTTP creation is included here; the focused
Ruby/Go fixtures still own their fresh-path and source-validation gates. This
harness also does not inspect every native outgoing RPC on the WebSocket, so
the fixtures that fail on fresh-path `thread/list` calls remain necessary.

If account/model access or the real standalone launch refuses this isolated
setup, preserve the evidence and report incomplete. Do not forge registration
evidence, fall back to a fake App Server, override catalog models, edit schemas,
or reuse production state. The smallest fallback is to run the prepared
focused command-boundary/protocol fixtures and defer the real timing gate to
the retained live portal canary; their evidence must be labelled separately.
